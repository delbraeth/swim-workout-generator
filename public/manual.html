Overview

SetForge creates structured swim workouts tailored to your pool, pace, available equipment, and time budget. Every workout is built from four sections — Warm-Up, Drill, Main Set, and Cool-Down — chosen from a library of options calibrated for masters swimmers.

🎯
9 Workout Types
IM, Distance, Sprint, Endurance, Technique, Mixed, plus Backstroke, Breaststroke, and Butterfly specialty workouts.
🔁
Repeat Sets
Main sets can automatically repeat 2–3 rounds with rest between, filling your yardage target efficiently.
⏱
Pace-Aware Intervals
All intervals scale to your personal 100-yard pace target. Adjust globally or per set.
🏊
25yd & 50m Pools
Separate option banks for 25-yard and 50-meter pools. Native 50m sets for most workout types.
📱
Run Mode
Step through sets one at a time with a full-screen pace clock, elapsed time, and auto-advance rest timer.
📊
History & Stats
Every saved workout is logged. Stats panel shows sessions, total yards, weekly rate, and type breakdown.
⭐
Favorites
Star your favorite main sets. They get extra weight in the generator and are pinned in your History.
☁️
Cloud Sync
Your workout history, favorites, and settings are stored in the cloud and sync across devices.

Quick Start

Sign in with Apple

Tap "Sign in with Apple" on the landing page. Your workouts and settings are tied to your Apple ID.

Choose a workout type

Select one of the nine type cards: IM, Distance, Sprint, Endurance, Technique, Mixed, or a stroke specialty.

Set your target distance

Drag the yardage slider or tap a time preset (~60 / ~90 / ~2hr). Enter your 100-yard pace to get accurate time estimates.

Choose equipment

Check off the gear you have today. The generator only uses sets compatible with what you've selected.

Generate

Tap 🏊 Generate Workout. Review the four sections. Hit regenerate on any section you want to swap out.

Run or save

Tap ▶ Run to step through sets in the pool, or Save to History to log it and print later.

Signing In

The app uses Sign in with Apple for authentication. Your Apple ID is never shared — only a permanent, anonymized identifier is used to link your workouts and settings.

Returning users

Just click Sign in with Apple on the gate screen. Your existing account is recognized by Apple ID; everything (history, favorites, settings) comes back exactly as you left it.

New users (invite required)

Access is invite-only. To join, you need an invite link from someone who already uses the app. Invite links look like:

https://setforge.io/?invite=CODE

Click the invite link, pick either Sign in with Apple or Sign in with Google, and you're in. The invite is consumed on first use; the same link won't admit a second account (unless the inviter configured it for multiple uses). If you see an invite_invalid, invite_expired, or invite_exhausted message, ask the inviter for a fresh link.

Sessions

Once signed in, your session persists via a secure cookie for 30 days. You can see all your active sessions and sign out from individual devices via the 👤 Account menu — see Profile (Account / Curation / Subscription).

Yardage & Pace

The Yardage Slider

Drag the slider to set your target maximum distance. The generator fills to within ~400 yards of this target, so a 2400yd target typically produces a 2000–2400yd workout.

Time presets

Tap ~60 min, ~90 min, or ~2hr to auto-set the slider based on your pace. The preset is calculated from your pace input, so set your pace first for accurate estimates.

Custom range (your personal min & max)

Click the left tick label (the minimum) or right tick label (the maximum) on the slider to edit your personal bounds. For example, if you never swim more than 3000yd, set the max to 3000 and it will stay there across sessions. Your custom range is saved to your account.

💡
One swimmer, different ranges. Two users can have completely different slider ranges — one at 1900–2400yd, another at 2400–4000yd. Each person's range persists independently.

Pace Input

Enter your comfortable 100-yard pace (format: M:SS, e.g. 2:00) in the pace field. This does two things:

  • Scales all intervals — every generated interval is adjusted from the masters baseline (2:00/100) to your pace.
  • Powers the time presets — the ~60/~90/~2hr buttons calculate the right yardage for your pace.

You can also rescale intervals after generation from within any workout block. Your default pace is saved to your account.

Generating a Workout

Once a workout type is selected and the slider is set, tap 🏊 Generate Workout. The generator:

  1. Picks a warm-up that fits within the yardage budget.
  2. Picks a drill set compatible with your equipment and warm-up.
  3. Picks a main set (possibly as a 2× or 3× repeat) that fills the remaining budget to within ~400 yards of your target. Favorited sets get extra weight; repeat variants get extra weight to fill higher yardage targets; main sets you completed in the last 7 days are down-weighted to 25% so you naturally see fresh variety.
  4. Picks a cool-down to close the workout.
  5. Scales all intervals to your pace setting.

The summary bar shows total yardage, estimated duration, and your target range. Tap 🔄 Generate New Workout at any time to replace everything.

🔁
"Last seen Nd ago" badge. If the generated main set was one you completed within the last 7 days, the block header shows a red last seen Nd ago pill (or last seen today). The down-weight makes recent repeats rare, but if one still shows up, just hit Regenerate on the main block for an alternative.

Focus Note

Above the workout blocks (on freshly generated workouts) is an optional 🎯 Focus input — a one-line intention for the workout, like hip rotation, strong turns, or negative split the 200s.

  • Saves with the workout when you log it.
  • Shown in the run-mode section header and at the top of the pace clock so you see it between sets.
  • Shown in the print header above the workout blocks.
  • For workouts loaded from history, the focus note is shown read-only — it's a snapshot of your intention at the time, not editable after the fact.

Different from the post-workout Notes field on the save form: focus note is your pre-workout intention; notes are your post-workout journal.

Quick-Launch

Above the Generate button, a Pick up where you left off panel shows up to 3 of your most recent completed workouts as compact cards.

  • Tap anywhere on a card → opens the rest-picker, then drops you straight into Run Mode for that workout. Two taps from app launch to swimming.
  • The small 🔁 button on each card loads it into the generator instead, where you can tweak before logging.
  • Hidden once you've generated or loaded a workout — it doesn't push the active workout off-screen.
  • The layout adapts to your screen: three cards side-by-side on desktop, stacked on phones.
💡
Quick-launch only counts your own workouts (filtered by your account), not workouts logged by others sharing the device.

Recovery Mode

Above the workout-type selector is an Easy day toggle pill. Click to flip it on — it turns green and reads Recovery day ON. The next workout you generate is dialed back for active-recovery effort.

What changes when onEffect
Main-set poolFiltered to options in the Easy or Aerobic effort zones only. If a type's mains are all higher-intensity, the filter falls back to the full pool and lets the other recovery levers do the work.
Repeat suppression×2 and ×3 main-set variants are skipped — you'll always get the base option, no multi-round wrapping.
Floor dropMinimum total drops from 1,900 yds (2,000 m) to 1,200 yds (1,300 m), AND the "stay close to your max" rule is suspended for the generation. Result: a recovery workout actually comes in short — e.g. with max=2,400 you can get a ~1,300 yd session instead of a forced ~2,000 yd one.
Interval stretchEvery set's interval is multiplied by 1.10 after pace scaling. On 2:00 becomes On 2:12.

The generated workout gets a green summary-bar border and a 🌿 Recovery day badge so you can tell at a glance. Regenerating a section on a recovery-day workout preserves the flag — the new section also follows recovery rules.

ℹ️
Per-generation only. The toggle isn't persisted to settings. Flip it off and the next generate is back to normal. For a longer-term shift toward easy days, set the Recovery training phase instead.

Effort Zones

Every workout block carries an effort-zone label — a small colored pill in the block header reading e.g. ● THRESHOLD. The five zones, from easiest to hardest:

ZoneColorTypical cues
Easy■ GreenRecovery, warm-up, cool-down, drill technique work
Aerobic■ BlueSteady, moderate, sustained — most distance work
Threshold■ Yellow"Comfortably hard" — descend sets, build sets, T-pace
VO2■ OrangeFast — race-pace 100s, fast finishers
Anaerobic■ RedAll-out — sprints, top speed, blast sets

Block-level zones reflect the dominant zone across all the block's sets, weighted by yardage. A 1,000-yard aerobic block with one 100-yard VO2 finisher reads as Aerobic at the block level — and the finisher set gets a small orange dot next to its Reps × Dist, flagging it as out-of-zone relative to its block.

Zones power three other features: the Recovery mode filter, the Intensity dashboard, and the Training phase generator bias.

Training Phase

A longer-term setting (weeks to months) that shapes the generator's main-set picks. Set it in Account → Goals → 📅 Training phase. Five phases:

PhaseWhat the generator emphasizes
🌱 BaseAerobic foundation. Boosts easy/aerobic mains; suppresses VO2/anaerobic.
🔨 BuildThreshold/VO2 emphasis. Boosts threshold (1.5×) and VO2 (1.3×); softens easy (0.8×).
⛰️ PeakRace-specific. Boosts VO2 and anaerobic (1.5× each); softens easy/aerobic.
📉 TaperReduce intensity, preserve sharpness. Easy (1.5×), aerobic (1.3×); cuts VO2 (0.5×) and anaerobic (0.3×).
🌿 RecoveryActive recovery only. Heavy boost to easy (2.0×) and aerobic (1.5×); strong cuts to everything threshold and above.

The phase appears as a soft colored badge next to the workout summary bar on every generate. Click None (or the currently-active phase button) to clear the phase — the generator runs without phase bias.

💡
Phase vs. Recovery mode. The Recovery phase is a longer-term setting that nudges every generate toward easier work. Recovery mode is a per-generation switch that hard-filters mains and stretches intervals for one workout. They compose: a swimmer in Recovery phase can still flip on Recovery mode for an extra-easy day.

Template Engine — Bank / Engine / Mix

Each section header has a [BANK][ENGINE][MIX] pill toggle that controls where its content comes from on the next generate or regen:

SourceWhat you get
Bank (default)A curated set from the hand-authored library — the original SetForge behavior.
EngineA set generated on the fly from a structural template (pyramid, descend ladder, sandwich, race-day simulation, etc.). Sized to fit the section's yardage, validated against an 8-rule check, and retried up to 3 times before falling back to bank.
MixA bank prefix + an engine suffix in the same section. Two labeled sub-blocks render under one section header. Total yardage exceeds the section's bank allocation by ~50% — Mix is opt-in extra variety, not a strict budget mode.

⚡ Engine badge

Engine-generated sections show a small amber ⚡ ENGINE badge in the section header. Hover the badge to see the template that produced the set (e.g. pyramid_up_down, block_with_recovery, race_day_simulation). The badge appears on the section header for pure Engine output, and on each sub-block divider for Mix output.

Anti-repeat memory

The engine remembers the last 10 (template, stroke) pairs you generated and avoids picking the same template-stroke combo twice in a row. If you toggle to Engine on a section where the last 10 generates already covered every applicable template, the engine silently falls back to the bank for that section.

Bank fallback

The engine isn't a guaranteed win — when validation retries fail or no template applies to your (type × section) combo, the section silently uses bank instead. No error, no surprise — you just see a regular bank label and no ⚡ badge. This is the safety net per spec §5.

Your toggle choice persists per-profile across logins and devices. Click a section's pin (📍) to lock it before regenerating others, exactly the same as bank-only mode.

Intervals

Each set shows a target interval in the rightmost column (e.g. On 2:15). These are pre-scaled to your pace setting. You can adjust them two ways:

⚠️
Base pace assumption. The bank's authored intervals are calibrated for a masters baseline of 2:00/100yd (~2:11/100m in SCM, slightly slower in LCM). When you set your own pace and click Rescale all, every interval is scaled by the ratio (your pace ÷ 2:00) so the relative effort matches. A 1:50 swimmer tightens everything ~8%; a 2:15 swimmer loosens everything ~12.5%. The catalog (coach view) shows the unscaled baseline; per-set editing here uses your scaled values.

Rescale all intervals

In the pace field above the slider, update your pace and the app rescales every interval in the current workout proportionally. This is useful when you want to try a harder or easier version of the same workout.

Edit a single interval

Click any interval in the workout table to edit it inline. Type the new interval, then press Enter or click away to commit. The estimated duration updates immediately.

The editor accepts any of these forms — they're all normalized to the canonical On M:SS when saved:

You typeStored asNotes
1:30On 1:30Standard M:SS — the most common form.
:45On :45Sub-minute intervals.
90On 1:30Bare integer = total seconds (range 5–3600).
no interval / none / offNo interval — swim easyRemoves the interval; the set's duration falls back to 35 yd/min.

Anything the editor can't parse (e.g. typos, decimals, hours) is rejected with an inline message, the input gets a red border, and the editor stays open so you can fix it. Press Escape to cancel without saving.

Clear an interval (⊘ button)

While editing, the ⊘ button next to the input clears the set's interval to "No interval — swim easy" in one click. Useful for switching a timed set to a free-swim recovery.

💡
Sets without intervals show "No interval" — these are typically easy recovery swims. The estimated duration for these sets uses a 35 yd/min fallback.
ℹ️
Estimated time includes transitions. The "~N min" estimate adds +10 seconds per between-set boundary (within blocks and between blocks) on top of the raw interval × reps math. A typical 4-block workout adds ~2 minutes for transitions. Old saved entries keep whatever estimate was computed at save time and won't recompute on load.

Set Descriptions

The description column in each set row is click-to-edit. Click any description text to open an inline editor, make your changes, then press Enter or click away. Changes flow through to the print layout, history save, and Run Mode.

Descriptions reset when you generate or regenerate a section.

Set Swap

The ⇄ button appears on individual sets within a block. Tap it to see a shorter and longer equivalent set. Swapping rescales the interval and updates the block's total yardage.

Use set swap to fine-tune the balance within a section without regenerating the whole block.

💡
Intervals scale non-linearly. Doubling distance doesn't simply double the interval — a 100 @ 2:00 turns into 200 @ ~4:10, not 4:00. That matches how swimmers actually slow as distance grows (Riegel-style ~1.06 exponent). For sprints, halving works the same way in reverse: the swap is a starting point — adjust ±5 sec to match your own effort.
ℹ️
Swap availability depends on the bank. Both shorter and longer options only appear when the workout bank for your current pool mode actually contains sets at the target distance. That filters out impossible distances like a 75-yd set halved to 37.5. SCY and SCM banks cover a wider range (25 distinct distances up to 1,500); LCM is sparser (7 distinct distances), so long-course swimmers see fewer swap options.
ℹ️
Multi-stroke sets aren't swappable. Sets whose description encodes a within-set breakdown — e.g. "25 Fly / 25 Back / 25 Breast" — won't show the ⇄ button. Halving or doubling those would leave the displayed description inconsistent with the actual reps × dist.

Repeat Sets

When the yardage target is high or when the generator determines it's the best fit, the main set may be selected as a repeat block — the same set performed 2 or 3 rounds with rest in between.

Repeat blocks are labeled with a round count (e.g. ×2 rounds, 30s rest between) and display each round separately with "Round 1 / Round 2" headers.

Editing rest between rounds

Click the ── 30s rest ── row between rounds to edit the rest duration inline. Enter a number of seconds and press Enter. In Run Mode, the rest timer between rounds uses this value.

ℹ️
Repeat vs. regenerate. Regenerating a repeat-set block replaces the entire set (including the round structure) with a different option. Use set swap or description editing to tweak a repeat block in place.

Favorites & Disfavorites

SetForge lets you bias the workout generator toward things you like and away from things you don't, at three different levels of granularity. The shorthand: ★ favorite = 3× pick weight, 👎 disfavor = 0.25× pick weight (or zero, see Hard-exclude mode below).

The tri-state toggle

Every main-set block in a generated workout has a three-segment toggle in its header: [ ★ Favorite / — Neutral / 👎 Disfavor ]. Tap a segment to set that state — a label can be in exactly one state at a time. The states are mutually exclusive: favoriting a label automatically clears it from your disfavorites list and vice versa.

On engine-generated main sets (badge ⚡), the same three-segment toggle appears, but it acts on the engine template (e.g. descend_ladder + stroke im), not on a label — engine labels are dynamic each generate.

The three tiers

You can favorite or disfavor at three levels. The picker honors all three simultaneously.

  • Bank labels — the workout's main-set label (e.g. "IM Descend Ladder"). 3× / 0.25× pick weight in the bank picker. Tri-state toggle on each generated bank-sourced main block.
  • Engine templates — a (template_id, stroke) pair (e.g. descend_ladder + im). 3× / 0.25× weight when the engine picks which template to invoke. Tri-state toggle on each generated engine-sourced main block (⚡ badge).
  • Individual sets — a specific set inside an option (e.g. a particular 4×200 threshold). 3× / 0.25× per matching set, multiplicative across multiple matching sets in one option. Cycling button (☆ → ★ → 👎 → ☆) on each set row, both inside a generated workout and in the Catalog (coach access).

Universal precedence rule

When the same label, set, or engine tuple is both favorited (by you OR by your coach via propagation) AND disfavored (by you OR your coach), the favorite wins. The picker applies the 3× boost and skips the 0.25× downweight. This is consistent across all three tiers — if it's a favorite anywhere in the effective list, it gets boosted; the disfavor is silently ignored for that item.

Hard-exclude mode

By default, disfavor is a downweight: 0.25× pick weight, so disfavored items still appear occasionally. If you want certainty — never pick this — flip the mode toggle in your Profile's Disfavorites panel from Downweight (0.25×) to Hard-exclude. In hard-exclude mode, disfavored items get weight 0 and won't be picked. The setting applies to all three tiers (labels, sets, engine templates).

Safety net: if hard-exclude would empty the pick pool (every applicable option is disfavored), the picker falls back to a uniform random pick from the excluded pool so you still get a workout. This is silent — no warning. The honest read: if you're hitting it often, your disfavor list is too aggressive.

From the Catalog (coach access)

The catalog shows a ☆ on every option row — warmup, drill, main, and cool-down — alongside the 🚩 Flag button. Tapping it favorites that option's label. Each set inside the expanded option also gets the cycling ☆ → ★ → 👎 → ☆ button per row, so coaches can curate at the per-set level without first generating a workout. Sets without an authored ID show a dim "·" placeholder.

Auditing what you've curated

Open your Profile to see two audit panels — Favorites and Disfavorites — that list everything you've curated across all three tiers, with one-click Remove per row. See those sections under Profile (Account / Curation / Subscription) for details.

Coach group propagation

If a coach has favorited or disfavored something, their picks silently propagate to every swimmer in their groups. The swimmer sees the picker bias but no UI indication that the coach's curation is in effect — their own audit panel still shows only what THEY personally curated. See Coach group propagation under Coach docs for the details.

Regenerating Sections

Each workout block has a ↺ regenerate button. Tapping it replaces only that section with a different option while keeping the other three sections unchanged.

The generator ensures the new section:

  • Has a different fingerprint from the current section (no immediate repeats).
  • Fits the remaining yardage budget.
  • Satisfies your equipment selection.
  • Gets its intervals scaled to your current pace.

If no valid alternative exists (e.g., the constraints are too tight), an inline error message appears explaining why. Try adjusting the max yardage or equipment selection.

⚠️
Regenerating clears the save status. Even if the workout was previously saved, a section regen marks it as unsaved. You'll be prompted to save again before printing.

Split Logging

Tap ✓ Lap on the Pace Clock whenever you touch the wall on a rep. The app records your actual time vs. the expected interval, shows you the delta, and rolls the splits up into the workout when you save.

What gets captured

Each lap records a small object:

  • Rep number within the set.
  • Actual seconds — wall-clock time elapsed since the rep started.
  • Expected seconds — the set's interval as displayed on the pace clock.
  • Delta — actual minus expected (negative = under-pace, positive = over).

If you don't tap Lap and the timer counts down to zero on its own, a split is still recorded with delta = 0 — you made the interval exactly.

Live feedback

After each lap, a small pill appears below the rep dots:

  • last −0:08 · 1:52 — your most recent delta (green if under, red if over, slate if on-pace) plus the actual time.
  • avg ∆ −0:04 · 3/8 — the running average across captured reps + the count so far. Only shown once you have 2+ laps.

Saving splits

At the workout-finish screen, the summary line shows N splits captured (in green) when any were recorded. The primary CTA changes to "Log as today's session (with N splits)". Tapping it creates a fresh history entry dated today with the splits attached as actual_splits on the workout payload.

ℹ️
Reset clears splits. If you tap ↺ Reset mid-set, the in-progress splits for that set are dropped — you're starting over. Splits already captured in earlier sets are preserved.
💡
Skip vs. Lap. Lap records a split and advances. Skip just advances. Use Skip when you've abandoned a rep and don't want the time recorded.
💡
Sets with no interval open a simplified view showing the set details without a countdown. Tap "Done" to advance.

Rest Timer

The rest timer controls how the app advances between sets in Run Mode. You choose the mode before starting (see Run Mode). In timed modes, a rest countdown appears on the pace clock screen after each set finishes — you can always tap Skip to advance early.

For repeat-set blocks, the rest between rounds uses the per-block rest value you set in the workout view (default 30s), regardless of your global rest timer setting.

🔔 Audio cues

In the Start Workout dialog (the rest picker) there's a 🔔 Audio cues toggle. When ON, the app plays:

  • Three short beeps at T-3, T-2, T-1 as the rest timer counts down.
  • A distinct higher-pitched "go" tone at T-0 when the rest ends and the next set starts.

Tones are synthesized in the browser (no asset download). Tapping the toggle to enable it plays a one-beep preview so you can confirm your phone's volume is up. The preference is saved per-account; default is ON.

📱
iOS Safari: phone-deck audio works because the AudioContext is primed when you tap ▶ Start in the rest picker. If you don't hear anything, double-check your phone isn't on silent mode (the silent switch overrides Web Audio on iOS).

✋ Lap button

Right under the Audio cues toggle in the Start Workout dialog, there's a ✋ Lap button toggle. It controls how the running timer behaves during sets:

  • ON (default): while a rep is timing, the big primary button is ✓ Lap. Tap it as you touch the wall each rep — the timer records your actual split time and shows you last delta and avg delta vs. the interval. Useful when you want a record of how you actually swam each rep.
  • OFF: the primary button becomes ⏸ Pause. You don't tap per rep — the timer auto-advances when it hits zero on each interval. The splits feedback pill disappears since every split would be on-time. Useful when you just want a quiet pace clock and don't care about per-rep splits.

Either way, the Skip → button stays available, and the auto-advance behavior at T-0 is unchanged. The preference is saved per-account; default is ON.

Plan an Intent for a Future Day

An intent is a placeholder for a workout you'll generate on the day. You capture the shape — workout type, target yardage, mix, recovery — without nailing down the specific sets. When you open the week view on the planned day, you generate the actual workout from your current bank state, preview it, and either save it as-is or regenerate for a different shuffle.

Two ways to plan one:

  • From the week view. On any empty future day, tap + Plan intent. Fill in the date, type, max yardage, mix (balanced / warmup-heavy / drill-heavy / long-main), recovery toggle, and an optional note. Tap Plan intent ▸.
  • From the generator. Configure the form for the kind of workout you want, then below the Generate Workout button tap 💾 Save as intent for later…. Pick a date and save. The current form state — type, yardage, mix, recovery — becomes the intent.

Intent rows appear in the week view with an amber border and an INTENT tag, with the target yardage labeled (target) to distinguish it from a finalized workout's actual total.

Why use an intent instead of generating now?

  • Bank freshness. The set bank is updated over time — generating on the day uses the latest options.
  • Variety. Avoids locking in the same shuffle twice when you're planning across a long horizon.
  • Less commitment. You're committing to "a long-main aerobic day" rather than to a specific workout you might want to change.

Impersonation (admin + support_role)

Customer-support tool. An admin or a user with the support_role flag can act AS another specific user for up to 30 minutes, seeing their actual favorites, history, groups, generated workouts — everything that user would see when logged in. Used for diagnosing user-reported issues without asking the user to share screen or hand over their credentials.

This is NOT the same as View as. View-as is a UI preview that hides coach/admin features so you can see what other roles see, using YOUR data. Impersonation is a true session swap — you see the OTHER user's actual data, and any reads are recorded against them in the audit log. Different tools for different jobs.

Who has the right

Either flag grants impersonation access:

  • is_admin — full admin already has the right. Toggled at Admin → Users → Edit (the underlying flag).
  • support_role — new flag for support staff who should be able to impersonate WITHOUT full admin rights (can't disable users, can't manage invites, can't grant other roles). Toggled at Admin → Users → Support. Admin-only to grant: support_role cannot grant support_role to other users.

Two entry points:

  • Admin → Users — each row shows a red 🛂 Impersonate button. Click → confirmation dialog → session starts. Best when you already know who you're impersonating.
  • Top-nav 🛂 button — opens a quick-start modal with live search by email, name, initials, or sub. Best for quick lookups when you've forgotten the exact name.

You cannot impersonate yourself. Starting a new session while one is already active automatically ends the prior session (one active session at a time per admin).

What you can and can't do

Impersonation is read-only. The server blocks all writes (POST/PUT/PATCH/DELETE) for the duration of the session except the explicit Exit endpoint. Specifically you CAN view:

  • The target's generator screen as they'd see it (their pace, equipment, type selection, etc.)
  • Their workout history and stats
  • Their favorites and disfavorites (and curation impact, if they're a coach)
  • Their group memberships, lane plans, assigned workouts

You cannot generate a workout, save anything, change their settings, or toggle their favorites. If you click a write control, the server returns 403 and the UI does nothing.

30-minute hard cap

Every session expires 30 minutes from start. The red banner at the top of the app shows a live countdown — when it hits zero, the session auto-ends and you're returned to your own account. To continue past 30 minutes, exit and start a new session (which generates a new audit row).

Click Exit on the banner at any time to end early. Exiting reloads the page so any cached target data is cleared.

Audit trail

Every API call made during an impersonation session is logged to audit_events with both your sub (impersonator_sub) and the target user's sub (user_sub). Event types: impersonation.start, impersonation.access (per request), impersonation.end. Filter the admin Audit log by impersonator_sub to review your own (or another admin's) impersonation activity.

User notification policy

Impersonated users are not notified when a session starts or ends. The audit log is the only record visible (only to admins). This is a deliberate v3.0 choice — the trade-off is that customer-support workflows can act without consent friction, at the cost of less user-side transparency. A v3.1 enhancement to surface impersonation events to the impersonated user on their next login is on the backlog.

State persistence

If you reload the page while a session is active, the banner reappears and the session continues — the client recovers state from /api/impersonation/active on auth. The 30-minute clock keeps ticking from when the session started, not from when you reloaded.

Limitations to know

  • Multi-tab — if you have SetForge open in two tabs and start impersonation in tab A, tab B's existing session stays unaffected (acts as your real account). Only tab A sends the impersonation header. Acceptable, but be aware that "what you see in this tab" may differ.
  • You're still in your groups — impersonating doesn't actually log you in as the target. Your own coach/admin permissions are still active for your own account in other tabs.
  • No revoke from another device — if you started a session from a laptop and want to end it from your phone, you'd need to wait for the 30-min expiry or sign in to the same admin session on the phone. v3.x could add a force-end-all-my-sessions endpoint if this becomes an issue.

Teams (coach access)

Teams are the institutional layer of the coaching relationship model. A team is a club / school / program / group of coaches that operates as one unit. Coaches own and join teams; groups (the actual training cohorts) live inside teams. Teams, groups, members, lane plans, assignments, claim flow, and coach notes are all live — the relationships scope is fully implemented end-to-end.

Access

A blue 👥 Teams button appears in the header for users with the is_coach flag (or is_admin, since admins implicitly have coach access). Granted via Admin → Users → Coach.

Team types

Each team has a type set at creation that drives compliance rules. Type is immutable after creation — to change it, archive and recreate.

  • High School — FERPA-shape: minor protections, restricted disclosure to non-parents, parental consent required for under-18 participants. Roster visibility forced off when minors are present.
  • Summer — Mixed-age seasonal program. Minor protections kick in only if under-18 members are added.
  • Club — Year-round mixed-age (USA Swimming pattern). Under-13 protections apply per member; over-13 are conventional.
  • Masters — Adults only (18+). Minimal compliance burden. If you accidentally add an under-18 to a Masters team, the system warns but does not block.

Roles within a team

Each coach in a team has one of three roles. Pills in the coach list are color-coded: purple owner, blue admin, slate coach.

  • Owner — created the team. Can rename / archive the team, add and remove coaches, and promote/demote between Admin and Coach. The owner cannot be removed via the UI (would orphan the team); ownership transfer is a planned future flow.
  • Admin — promoted by the owner. Can manage team coaches and archives. Promote/demote from the Coach pill using the ↑ Admin arrow that appears for the owner on each row.
  • Coach — can operate within groups they're assigned to. Read-only at the team level.

Creating and managing

The Teams page shows all teams you're a member of with your role on each. To create a team, click + New team, enter a name, pick a type, and save — you become the owner. To open a team, click its row; the detail view shows the type, coach list, and (if you're the owner) controls to rename, archive, and add/remove coaches.

To add a coach, pick them from the dropdown (only users with the is_coach flag appear) and choose a role (Coach default, or Admin for team-management privileges). After adding, an owner can promote or demote any coach with the ↑ Admin / ↓ Coach arrow on each row.

Removing a coach who's primary on groups: if you try to remove a coach who's still the primary on any active group in this team, you'll see a Transfer primary first modal listing each affected group. Pick a new primary for each from the team's other coaches, then click Transfer & remove ▸ — the transfers run in sequence and the removal completes atomically.

Archive behavior

Archiving a team is soft and reversible — click the button again to unarchive. Per the relationships scope, team archive does NOT cascade to groups — archiving a team detaches its groups as independent (still-active) groups rather than archiving them too. Groups have their own archive flow.

Groups (Stage 2)

Inside a team's detail view, the 🏊‍♀️ Groups section (amber-accented) lists training cohorts in this team. Team owners and admins can create new groups; group primary coaches manage members, sub-coaches, the training phase, and roster visibility.

  • Create — name + optional default pool mode (SCY/SCM/LCM). You become the group's primary coach automatically.
  • Expand any group row to see members + coaches + phase setter + settings.
  • Members — pick from your team-affiliated managed swimmers (set their team in 🏊 Managed swimmers first). Each swimmer joins as primary or secondary. A swimmer can be a primary in only one of your groups at a time (e.g., their main training group); they can be secondary in any number (dryland, test-set crew, etc.). Full-account swimmers (with their own login) join via the code-based Invite full-account swimmers flow below.
  • Group coaches — you (primary) can add assistant coaches via the dropdown. Assistants can see the group + manage members but can't archive or change settings.
  • Training phase — 5-pill toggle (Base / Build / Peak / Taper / Recovery). Lands on every member; when you generate for this group, the group's phase overrides each coach's personal phase. Last-changed timestamp recorded.
  • Roster visible to members — toggle that lets group members see each other's names. Forced off (and the server rejects enabling) when any active member is under 18. Privacy gate, no override.
  • Archive group — soft-archive; auto-stamps left-at on all active members at archive time. Team archive does NOT cascade to groups (per scope decision #13).

Team events (decision #38)

Below the Groups section, 📅 Events (amber) is a team-level calendar of upcoming events (meets, championships, training trips, etc.). Any team coach can add or delete events. v1 captures just name + date.

Upcoming events surface on the main app via the pool-mode selector row: a 📅 amber pill showing the most-imminent event across all teams you're in (as either a coach or a group member). Renders alongside your personal 🏁 next-event pill — both coexist; team events take the leading position.

Assigned to me

When a coach generates a workout for a group you're in, it lands in your 📥 Assigned to me view (teal button in the top nav, visible to all authenticated users — you don't need to be a coach to use it). Filter the list by completion state (Not started / Partial / Complete / Missed / All).

Each row shows the workout type + total yardage, the coach who assigned it, the group + lane info if applicable, and your current completion state. Click a row to expand it inline — you'll see the full workout blocks plus action buttons.

To do the workout: click ▶ Run this workout. The standard Run mode opens with the coach's snapshot. When you finish, the completion lands on the assignment row (not as a new workout in your history) — splits, difficulty, and any focus note are captured.

To log without running (offline / skipped): use the inline ✓ Mark complete, ◐ Mark partial, or ✕ Mark missed buttons. Mark partial / missed both accept an optional reason note.

Coach-mark-on-behalf (Flow N)

Inside any group's expanded view, coaches see a Recent assignments subsection listing every assignment that's been fanned out to group members. From the deck, the coach can mark any swimmer's completion state (✓ complete / ◐ partial / ✕ missed) without waiting for the swimmer to log it themselves. The swimmer-side display shows a "(by coach)" tag on coach-marked completions so they know it wasn't them.

Inviting full-account swimmers

To bring a swimmer with their own login account into a group (vs the managed-profile path), use the code-based join flow. Per the scope's code-only decision (#6), the app generates a short opaque code; the coach hands it to the swimmer out-of-band (text message, in person, email — whatever's convenient); the swimmer redeems it inside the app.

Coach side

In a group's expanded view (primary coach only, group must not be archived):

  1. Invite full-account swimmers subsection → + Issue join code
  2. Pick role on join: primary (their main training group) or secondary (additional, e.g., dryland)
  3. Click Issue code → 10-character lowercase code displayed prominently
  4. Copy button → share the code with the swimmer out-of-band
  5. Code is valid for 30 days. Outstanding (unredeemed) codes show below; primary coach can Revoke any that haven't been redeemed yet.

Swimmer side

Anyone with an authenticated account (not just coaches) can join groups they've been invited to:

  1. 👤 Profile modal → scroll to Join a group section
  2. Paste the code, click Look up → preview shows which group + what role
  3. Join group → membership created instantly

DOB gate: the first time a swimmer joins any group, they're prompted to enter their date of birth (inline, on the redeem screen). This is required for minor-protection compliance. Once entered, future joins skip this step.

One-primary-per-coach rule (decision #33): a swimmer can be a primary in only one of a given coach's groups at a time. If a coach issues a primary-role code and the swimmer is already a primary in another of that coach's groups, the redemption errors with the conflicting group's name and guidance: leave the other group first, or ask the coach for a secondary-role code instead.

Lane plans

Inside an expanded group with 2+ members, primary coaches see a Lane plans section. Lane plans are reusable per-group configurations of swimmers into named lanes with target paces (e.g. "Tuesday config: Sprint @ 1:50, Aerobic @ 2:05, Recovery @ 2:20"). Each plan stores: name, default flag, and an ordered list of lanes (label + target pace + member chips).

Build a plan: click + New plan, name it, then for each lane: set label + target pace, then add swimmers from the group via the per-lane dropdown. A swimmer can be in at most one lane per plan. Click + Add lane to append more lanes. Save when done. A plan can be marked default — only one plan per group can be default at a time.

Use a plan at generation: on the generator's "Generate for" picker, when you pick a multi-member group that has lane plans, a second dropdown appears: Lane plan: [default plan / any other plan / No plan]. Default plan is preselected when you pick the group. Generating with a plan fans out assignments per-lane — each swimmer gets an assignment row tagged with their lane index, label, and target pace (the lane's pace, not their stored pace). Generating with "No plan" falls back to all current members at their stored pace (R-D behavior).

Stale members: swimmers in a plan but no longer in the group are skipped at generation time. Members currently in the group but not placed in any lane of the selected plan are also skipped — the plan is the authoritative member list for that generation.

Generate for a group

On the generator page, coaches see a Generate for picker above the big Generate button. Default is Myself (no assignment created, your normal solo flow). Two other choices:

  • Private students — groups with exactly one member. Listed by swimmer name. Generating saves the workout to your history AND creates a single assignment record pointing at that swimmer.
  • Groups — multi-member groups. Generating saves the workout to your history AND fans out one assignment per current active member (managed swimmers and full-account swimmers alike — the polymorphic membership model handles both). Each assignment snapshots the member's stored pace for the workout's pool mode (or, with a lane plan, the lane's pace).

When a group is selected, the group's current training phase overrides your personal phase for that generation (decision #39). The workout-display header shows an amber → Group X (N) pill confirming the fanout target; this pill persists in your history so you can see which past workouts were group-assigned.

The workout itself lives in your history (you're the owner). Assignments are stored separately; each swimmer sees the workout in their own 📥 Assigned to me view and logs completion against the assignment row (splits, difficulty, focus note all captured back to the assignment, not as a separate workout in their history).

Multi-pace print (coach + group only)

When generating for a group with ≥2 members, an amber 🏊 Multi-pace print… button appears next to the Print Workout button. Click it to open the multi-pace modal:

  • Source — pick a saved lane plan (default selected if one exists) or switch to Manual entry and type per-lane paces by hand.
  • Render — choose between Per-lane pages (one print page per lane, each rendering the full workout at that lane's pace, intervals rescaled from the 2:00/100 baseline) or Matrix view (single page with N pace columns alongside the workout structure).
  • Preview & Print — closes the modal, mounts a full-screen print overlay, auto-fires the browser print dialog. Use the close button to dismiss.

Per-lane pages is the intended workflow: print once, hand one page to each lane on the deck. Matrix mode is more compact but harder to read on deck.

Mix pills (section bias)

Above the workout type selector, a Mix: pill row lets you bias yardage allocation between sections per-generation (session-only — not persisted to settings). Four modes:

  • Balanced — default. Roughly 13% warmup / 17% drill / 65% main / 5% cooldown.
  • Warmup-heavy — bigger warmup (~22%), main reduced (~62%).
  • Drill-heavy — bigger drill (~26%), main reduced (~61%).
  • Long main — most yardage on the main set (~83%), warmup + drill suppressed.

Effectiveness varies by workout type and total yardage — the allocator can only bias toward target yards within what the bank offers. At very large workout sizes (3K+), the bank's biggest single-piece warmups and drills may cap below the target; the generator falls back to the closest available option.

Managed Swimmers (coach access)

A managed swimmer is a roster entry you (the coach) own — name, paces, contact info — for someone who doesn't have their own full-account login. Think of it as a lightweight athlete record under your care. Once Teams and groups are wired up, managed swimmers can be members of your groups alongside full-account swimmers.

The Managed Swimmers view is a first-class top-level roster — it shows all your managed profiles regardless of group placement, so you can build the roster before assigning anyone to a group.

Access

A cyan 🏊 Managed swimmers button appears in the header for users with the is_coach flag (or is_admin). Granted via Admin → Users → Coach.

Why DOB is required

Every managed profile must have a date of birth. The DOB drives derived compliance properties — minor (under 18) and COPPA-protected (under 13) — which gate downstream features like the claim flow (under-13 swimmers cannot be claimed into a full account) and roster visibility (groups containing minors force roster-visibility off). Minor status is computed live, never stored: a swimmer's age changes silently as the calendar advances.

DOB itself is private. Other coaches and admins only see the swimmer's age and a minor/adult pill; the raw date is visible only to the owning coach.

Fields

  • Display name (required) — how the swimmer appears in lists.
  • Initials (optional, max 4 chars) — used in compact UIs.
  • DOB (required) — date of birth. Bounded between 1900-01-01 and today.
  • Paces (optional) — per pool mode (SCY / SCM / LCM), in M:SS format. Coach enters their best knowledge of the swimmer's 100-yard/100-meter cruise pace.
  • Parental contact (optional) — email or phone for the swimmer's parent or guardian. Recommended for under-18 swimmers in high-school teams.
  • Parent-managed flag — for club-type teams, lets the coach mark a 13+ profile as parent-controlled. Setting this flag blocks the claim flow even though the age threshold has passed.

Browsing

The roster shows non-archived swimmers by default; toggle Show archived to include archived rows. Each card shows display name, age + DOB, optional SCY pace, and a colored minor pill (red "under 13" / amber "minor" / grey "adult"). Click any card to open the detail view.

Editing & archive

From the detail view, click ✎ Edit to update any field including DOB (validation is re-applied; out-of-bounds dates are rejected). The Archive button soft-hides the swimmer without deleting any data — toggle back to restore. Per the scope, no managed-swimmer record is ever hard-deleted by the coach (claim migration is the only path that removes the row, and only after repointing all dependent data to the new full-account swimmer).

What managed swimmers can't do

  • Log in — managed profiles have no auth identity. The coach is the operator for all their data. The claim flow (below) is the only path to give a managed profile its own login.

Claim link — managed → full-account migration

When a swimmer creates their own account (Apple Sign-In) and wants to take over the managed profile you've been maintaining for them, issue a claim link. The redeemed claim atomically migrates the managed profile's data into their account and deletes the managed row.

Coach side

In the swimmer's detail view, scroll to the Claim link (managed → full-account) panel:

  1. Click Send claim link → confirmation dialog explains what happens on redeem (identity fields silently overwrite, all dependent rows repoint, managed profile deleted).
  2. 10-character lowercase code displayed prominently. Use Copy and share it out-of-band (text, email, etc.).
  3. Code is valid for 30 days. Outstanding codes show below with a Revoke button per code.

Blocked when: the swimmer is under 13 (COPPA-protected — claim is hard-blocked, panel shows a red notice) or the parent-managed flag is set (uncheck the flag on the swimmer detail first, then retry).

Swimmer side

In their 👤 Profile modal, scroll to Claim a managed profile:

  1. Paste the 10-character code → click Look up → preview shows the managed swimmer's name and the issuing coach's name.
  2. Click Confirm claim. The atomic migration runs server-side: name / DOB / gender are copied from the managed profile onto the swimmer's account (silently overwriting per scope decision #24); all workout assignments, coach notes, and group memberships repoint to the swimmer's sub; the managed profile is deleted.
  3. A post-claim diff screen confirms exactly which identity fields changed.

Group conflict (#21/#33): if the managed profile is primary in a group whose coach also has the swimmer as primary in another group, the redemption errors with both group names. The swimmer must resolve the conflict manually (leave one of the groups) before the claim can proceed.

Coach notes (private journal)

Each managed swimmer's detail view has a Coach notes panel — a timestamped journal of observations, attribution, edits, and reminders. Multiple notes per swimmer, newest-first.

  • Visibility — each note carries one of three scopes:
    • Private — only you (the author) see it.
    • Group coaches — visible to other coaches on the swimmer's primary group with you.
    • Team coaches — visible to all active coaches on the swimmer's team.
  • Default visibility — derived from team type. High-school teams default to Team coaches (the team-shared default is signaled with a banner above the create form); all other types default to Private. You can change visibility per note.
  • Edit / delete — only your own notes show edit/delete buttons. Other coaches' notes are read-only with the author name + visibility pill displayed.
  • Soft-delete — deleted notes are hidden from lists but the row is preserved (deleted_at timestamp stamped). No hard-delete from the UI.

Notes survive a claim — when the swimmer redeems a claim link, all notes pinned to the managed profile repoint to the new full-account swimmer.

DOB soft-prompt for your own account

If you signed up before DOB was required (most existing accounts), you'll see a Quick question — your date of birth modal on next app load. Enter it and Save, or tap Remind me later. The prompt is dismissable for the current browser tab; it reappears on the next session. Once you've entered your DOB, the modal stops appearing entirely. Hard gates that require DOB (joining a group, accepting a coach invite) will re-prompt unconditionally when those features ship in later stages.

Team affiliation (optional)

Each managed swimmer can be affiliated with one of your teams. The team picker on the create/edit form lists every active team you're a coach of; pick one or leave it as "no team" for free-floating roster entries (private students, prospects, swimmers you haven't placed in an org yet). The picker defaults to your team automatically if you only have one. Team affiliation is independent of group membership — when groups land in a later stage, a swimmer can be on a team without yet being in any group.

The same picker appears at the top of the bulk-import modal: all swimmers in that import run get attached to the selected team. To import swimmers for two different teams, run the import twice with different team selections.

Gender (optional)

Each managed-swimmer record has an optional Gender field with values Male, Female, Non-binary / other, or Prefer not to say. Unset is also valid (the field is optional). Gender is used for heat assignment when groups land in later stages, and is the same PII tier as DOB — visible to other coaches via roster views, raw value only writable by the owning coach (or the swimmer themselves via Profile, once that surface ships for full-account swimmers). Bulk import accepts a gender column with tolerant spellings (M / F / X / Male / Female / Non-binary / PNTS / Decline / etc.).

Bulk import (📥)

For onboarding a real roster, use the 📥 Import button at the top of the swimmers view. It accepts CSV or TSV in two ways: 📁 Choose CSV file uploads a file directly; or paste content into the textarea. Either way the same parser runs.

Required columns: name, dob.
Optional columns: initials, pace_scy_100, pace_scm_100, pace_lcm_100, parental_contact.

Headers are case-insensitive; column order doesn't matter; extra unknown columns are ignored. DOB accepts YYYY-MM-DD or MM/DD/YYYY (both work — Excel exports often use one or the other). Click 📄 Download template for a starter CSV with the header row pre-filled.

Flow: upload or paste → Parse → preview table shows each row with a green ✓ (valid) or red ✗ (validation error, reason inline) → Import N swimmers commits the valid rows. The server is per-row atomic — one bad row doesn't roll back the others; the result screen shows server-side rejections (rare; mainly DB-level constraint hits) alongside the success count.

Cap at 500 rows per import call. Larger rosters: split the file and run it in batches.

Workout Catalog (coach access)

The Catalog is a coach-gated read-only browse of the workout-set bank as authored. Use it to review what content is available per pool mode, audit interval calibration, or print a reference sheet before practice.

Access

A blue 📚 Catalog button appears in the header for users with the is_coach flag (or is_admin, since admins implicitly have coach access). Granted via Admin → Users → Coach.

Filters

The catalog has five compositional filters (all combine via AND):

  • Mode — 25y / 25m / 50m. The catalog shows what's actually authored for the selected pool — no cross-unit conversion or fallback. SCY and SCM tend to be similarly populated; LCM is sparser.
  • Section — Warmup / Drill / Main / Cooldown.
  • Type — All / im / distance / sprint / endurance / technique / mixed / back / breast / fly. Only meaningful for Drill and Main sections (Warmup and Cooldown are flat across all types).
  • Equipment — multi-select chips (kickboard / fins / paddles / pull buoy / snorkel). Filters to options whose sets use any of the selected equipment.
  • Zone — multi-select chips by effort zone (Easy / Aerobic / Threshold / VO2 / Anaerobic). Zone is inferred from set descriptions and intervals.
  • Search label — text input, live filters by case-insensitive substring on the option's label. Press Escape to clear.

Browsing

Each row shows the option's label, type badge (for typed sections), zone pill, total yardage, and set count. A red ⚠ sum mismatch badge appears if the declared totalYards doesn't equal Σ reps × dist (a data-quality flag). Click a row to expand the full set list with reps × dist, interval, description, equipment badges, and focus.

Each option row also has a ☆ Favorite button that label-favorites the option (3× pick weight) and a 🚩 Flag button to send to feedback. Both work for warmup / drill / main / cool-down rows.

Per-set cycling button

Inside an expanded option, each set row gets a leading ☆ → ★ → 👎 → ☆ cycling button that toggles that specific set in/out of your favorites and disfavorites. Tap once to favorite, again to disfavor, again to clear. Mutex applies — a set can't be both at once. Sets without an authored set ID show a dim "·" placeholder (older bank entries that haven't been backfilled with IDs).

This is the same per-set state surfaced in the cycling button on each SetRow inside a generated workout — they read and write the same underlying favorites/disfavorites lists. Curate from the catalog before generating, or curate after generating; same outcome.

Flag for review

Each option row has a small 🚩 Flag button on the right. Click it to open the feedback modal pre-filled with the option's identity (label, pool mode, section, type, index, total yardage, set count) and a "What to review" prompt. Add your note and submit — the flag lands in the admin Feedback tab under the 🚩 Catalog flag category for triage. Useful for surfacing wrong intervals, misleading labels, mis-categorized workouts, etc.

Print catalog

The 🖨 Print catalog (expanded) button forces every visible row to expand, then opens the browser print dialog. Filters are still applied — to print the entire bank, clear filters first. Each option keeps together on one page where possible (no mid-option page breaks).

Coach: Planning for a Group

The intent flow (see Plan an Intent) doubles as a group planning tool for coaches. When you plan an intent and pick a group instead of "Yourself," the intent is scheduled for the group on that day. When you generate from it later, the resulting workout is automatically assigned to every member of the group — they'll see it in their 📥 Assigned to me view.

Planning a group intent

Coach group propagation (fav/disfavor)

Anything you favorite or disfavor — at any of the three tiers (bank labels, individual sets, engine templates) — silently propagates to every swimmer in every group you coach. Your swimmers see the picker bias take effect but don't see any UI hint that the curation came from you; their own Favorites and Disfavorites panels in Profile show only what THEY personally curated.

Scope

  • Both directions of curation propagate. Your favorites and your disfavorites both reach your group swimmers.
  • Both coach roles propagate. Primary AND assistant coaches contribute to their swimmers' effective list. (Internally: every active row in group_coaches, regardless of role.)
  • One-way only. The flow is coach → swimmer, never swimmer → coach. Your own swimmers' fav/disfavor lists don't bias your picks.
  • Universal favorite-wins precedence applies. If you disfavor an item and a swimmer favorites it themselves (or another coach favorites it), the favorite wins for that swimmer — they get the 3× boost, your downweight is suppressed for them.

Refresh cadence

Swimmers' apps refresh their effective fav/disfavor list every 5 minutes while they're signed in. If you toggle a curation, expect propagation to reach an actively-using swimmer within the window. A page reload also picks up the change immediately.

Auditing rollout

The Curation impact panel in your Profile shows per-item reach + workout-count numbers for the last 30 days, so you can see which of your curations are actually landing. Aggregate counts only; no per-swimmer breakdown.

📊 Reports

Reports lives behind the 📊 button in the top nav, visible to all signed-in users. The tabs you see depend on your role:

  • Coaches get four tabs: Programming Mix, Schedule Adherence, Curation Log, and Program Recap.
  • Solo / masters swimmers get one tab: Program Recap (R4 — your own training rollup).
  • Admins get two additional tabs: Platform Health and Curation & Support.

Self-only across the board — every report shows YOUR data; cross-coach comparison comes in a later phase.

Filters

  • Range — Last 7 days · Last 30 days (default) · Last 90 days · Season-to-date (anchored to Sept 1).
  • Group — pick one of your coached groups, or "All groups." Narrows R1 (Programming Mix) and R2 (Schedule Adherence) to workouts tied to that group. R3 (Curation Log) ignores the group filter — your curation is account-wide.

📈 Programming Mix (R1)

What you've been programming, broken down. Total yards · workout count · distinct bank labels used · Bank/Engine/Mix source share, plus three tables: yards by workout type (IM / Distance / Sprint / Endurance / Specialty etc.), yards by stroke (free / back / breast / fly / IM / kick / choice), and yards by section (warmup / drill / main / cooldown / recovery). Each table has a horizontal bar showing share of total. Useful for catching imbalance — too much sprint, no breaststroke for three weeks, etc.

🗓 Schedule Adherence (R2)

What you scheduled vs. what actually got swum. Stat cards: scheduled count, completed count + %, practices with attendance recorded + %, average attendance % per practice. Requires 📋 Mark practice done to be filled in for accurate "with attendance" numbers — if you stamp practices done without filling the roster, R2 will warn you with a count of attendance-less completions.

When a group is selected, R2 also shows roster trend: members joined vs. left in the range.

🎚 Curation Log (R3)

Every favorite + disfavorite you've set in the selected range, with timestamps. Three subsections: bank labels, set IDs, engine tuples. Each subsection has a Favorites column and a Disfavorites column. Engine tuples don't have per-item timestamps (they live in your settings JSON as a single list) — that subsection shows current state only.

📖 Program Recap (R4)

Available to all users (solo / masters / coaches). Personal training rollup over the selected range — no group dimension. Stat cards: total yards, workout count, multi-lane fit success rate (% of multi-lane workouts that satisfied every lane without falling back), and a count of any 30-day windows where you missed one of the four strokes. Below that, yards-by-type and yards-by-stroke tables (same bar-fill style as R1), and most/least-used bank labels and engine templates so you can see what you lean on vs. what's been gathering dust.

The 4-stroke gap detector slides a 30-day window through your range in 7-day steps. Any window missing free / back / breast / fly shows up in the warning table at the bottom — useful for catching "I haven't done breaststroke in over a month" without manually scrolling history.

🩺 Platform Health (R5, admin only)

Cross-team operational view. Stat cards for active coaches over the last 7/14/30 days (any coach who generated a workout). Below that: weekly bar-fill tables for workouts-per-week and engine fallback rate trend, plus feature-adoption percentages (% of workouts using engine, mix, multi-lane, fallback rate, count of users with favorites/disfavorites set vs. total user count). Optional team filter narrows the active-coaches + per-team breakdown.

🛡 Curation & Support Activity (R6, admin only)

Three sections. Propagating disfavor by team: per-team table showing how many disfavor items (labels + sets + engine tuples) the team's coaches collectively propagate to their swimmers — totals above 30 are flagged amber as a "this team is heavily curating" signal. (This is a simplified proxy; the true {">"} 30% effective-bank-reduction calculation is on the backlog.) Impersonation activity: per support actor, sessions count + average session minutes + distinct targets in range. Useful for review of support-session hygiene. Audit events per team: per-team rollup chips showing event_type ×count — quick scan for which teams are generating CSRF rejects, rate-limit hits, etc.

📄 Export (Print/PDF · Markdown)

Coach reports R1-R3 and the solo Program Recap (R4) have export buttons next to the range filter. Two formats:

  • 📄 Print / PDF — opens a print-friendly view in the same window with a print dialog. Use your browser's "Save as PDF" option in the print dialog to get a one-file PDF. The print view strips the chrome and renders just the report content with simple typography that works well on paper.
  • 📋 Markdown — downloads a .md file you can paste into email, Slack, Notion, etc. Markdown export is available for all 6 reports (including admin Platform Health and Curation & Support — useful for issue threads or status updates).

Admin reports (R5/R6) don't have a Print/PDF button — they're internal-use dashboards with no external-share use case. Markdown still works for them if you need to paste numbers into an internal doc.

  • No charts (stacked bars, pies, heatmaps) — numbers and tables only in v1. Charts deferred to a later phase.
  • No cross-coach comparison ("how does my Coach A compare to my Coach B"). Head-coach oversight is a v2 feature.

Multi-lane generate (coach access)

The companion to Multi-pace print: instead of taking an existing workout and printing it per lane, this generates a NEW workout that's chosen to fit every lane's pace from the start. Coach-only, gated on is_coach.

Where it lives

Below the yardage slider in the generator, you'll see a Multi-lane control with an OFF/ON toggle. When ON, the single pace input expands to N lane rows; you type a pace per lane. If a group is selected and it has a default lane plan, a Use lane plan button prefills the rows from the plan's lanes.

What changes when it's ON

  • The picker filters options to only those whose intervals fit ALL the lane paces. Specifically: per set, every lane's rest must be ≥ 5 seconds (slowest lane needs to breathe) AND ≤ 1.5× swim time (fastest lane shouldn't idle).
  • The engine validator applies the same lane-fit rule to engine-generated output.
  • On successful Generate, the multi-pace print view opens automatically with the new workout rendered at each lane's pace.

Lane-fit fallback banner

When your lane range is wide enough that the picker can't find lane-friendly options for one or more sections, it falls back to the unfiltered pool for those sections and shows a yellow ⚠️ banner: "Lane range is wide — the picker couldn't find options that fit every lane's pace for: [sections]. Those sections used the unfiltered pool, so intervals may be tight for your slowest lane or loose for your fastest." Tighten the lane range or relax the intervals to remove the warning.

Persistence

The Multi-lane toggle state and your lane rows are saved per-account (via settings.extra.multi_lane) — they survive page reload and follow you across devices. Up to 12 lanes per setup.

History

Multi-lane workouts save to history with the lane configuration embedded. History rows display a blue 🏊 N-lane chip when present; tooltip shows the paces (e.g. "Generated for 3 lanes: 1:30 / 1:45 / 2:00"). Loading the workout from history restores the multi-lane state and the lane rows — useful for re-running the same configuration without retyping.

Quick Input (coach shorthand)

Above the Generate Workout button there's a ✏️ Quick input… button that opens a modal where you can type a coach-style one-liner. The parser extracts what it recognizes, shows you the parsed pieces as chips, and applies them to the form when you click Apply. Designed for coaches who think in shorthand — no need to navigate the workout-type chips, slider, and pace input separately.

Behavior

  • Distance vs duration: if both are present, distance wins (more direct).
  • Multiple types: the first parsed type wins. "im technique" → IM is selected, "technique" shows in the chip preview.
  • Equipment: set to preferred (×2 weight in the picker), not required. Promote to required manually if you need a hard filter.
  • Sprint: matched as a workout type, not a zone (since "sprint workout" is the more common usage).
  • Out-of-range values: paces below 0:30 or above 5:00, distances below 500 or above 10000, durations below 15 min or above 240 min are silently ignored — they show up in the unparsed remainder.
  • Connector words like with / and / a / the / of / on / at / for / to are stripped from the unparsed remainder. Unknown words remain visible so you can see what wasn't recognized.

Tips & Tricks

Getting repeat sets to appear

Repeat sets are more likely at higher yardage targets (2400yd+). They get 2× weight in the pick pool. If you want repeats specifically, generate at 2400–3000yd and hit regenerate on the main set a few times until one appears.

Building a consistent routine

Star your favorite main sets. Over time, the generator will favor them — you get the variety of randomized warm-ups, drills, and cool-downs, but your main set stays in familiar territory.

Customizing before the pool

Generate at home, then use per-set interval editing and description editing to dial in exactly what you want. Copy to clipboard and text it to your lane partner, then save before you leave.

50m pool tips

Switch to 50m mode before generating. The slider will shift to 2000–4000m range. For IM or stroke specialty workouts, you'll see a notice that distances are in yards — these types don't yet have native 50m options.

Tracking multiple swimmers

Each Apple ID gets its own workout history, favorites, and settings. Use the Initials field on each save to identify who logged which workout — the History "Who" filter will then let you view each person's workouts separately.

Sharing a workout

The fastest path: generate → 📋 Copy Text → paste into Messages or Notes. For a nicer format: generate → save → 🖨 Print → save as PDF from the print dialog.
