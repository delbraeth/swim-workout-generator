# SetForge — Demo Narration Script (spoken voiceover)

The actual words to be spoken, keyed to the scene IDs in `DEMO_WALKTHROUGH.md`.
Tone: instructional + conversational, benefits and privacy woven in. Lines are
written to be **read aloud** — no emojis, abbreviations spoken in full on first
use, short speakable sentences. One block per clip.

# Run A — Head Coach / Team

**A1 — Landing & sign-in**
> Welcome to SetForge. If you coach swimming, you know the grind isn't the
> coaching — it's everything around it: writing practices, tracking who's in which
> group, and keeping parents in the loop. SetForge pulls all of that into one place.
> Let's start completely from scratch and build a team together.
> Signing in uses Apple or Google, so there's no password for us to store, and no
> password for you to lose. You're always in control of the account.

**A2 — Coach home**
> This is your coach home. Think of it as mission control. Your roster, your groups,
> your saved sets, your teams, practices, the shared catalog, and your reports — all
> one click away. The generator stays out of the way until you actually want to
> build a workout, so this screen stays calm.

**A3 — Build the team**
> Let's create a team. I'll call it Riverside Aquatics, and mark it as a club.
> In the team settings, I'll set a short team code — Riverside Aquatics becomes
> R-I-V. That code is what shows up when we export a roster to meet software later,
> so it pays to set it once.
> I'll also add our pool as a facility, with its address. SetForge geocodes it and
> drops it on a map — handy for away families, and it sets us up for travel and
> weather features down the road.

**A4 — Create a group**
> Teams are made of groups. I'll add a Senior Squad group, set to short course
> yards. Groups are how we assign one workout to a whole lane of swimmers at once,
> and they're the backbone of the team calendar we'll see in a minute.

**A5 — Import athletes**
> Now the part coaches dread — entering the roster. SetForge takes a simple
> spreadsheet. I'll paste eight athletes right here. All we truly need is a first
> name and a date of birth; everything else is optional. Watch the preview confirm
> each swimmer before anything is saved — nothing is committed until you say so.
> A quick word on the youngest swimmers: SetForge automatically flags minors, and
> swimmers under thirteen get extra protection by design, in line with children's
> privacy rules. You'll see those flags on the roster.

**A6 — Add athletes to the group**
> I'll drop our imported swimmers into the Senior Squad. Group membership is what
> drives both the workouts we assign and the schedule families can follow.

**A7 — Generate & assign**
> Here's the heart of it. I'll generate a workout — pick the focus, set the
> distance, and SetForge builds a structured, sensible practice in seconds. Then I'll
> assign it to the whole Senior Squad. Notice it lays out paces lane by lane, so
> every swimmer gets the right intervals from one click.

**A8 — Events & taper anchors**
> Let's add some events. A Spring Invitational — tagged as a meet. And Picture Day —
> tagged as picture day. Each event type gets its own icon so the calendar reads at
> a glance. And notice: only the meet offers a target anchor. That's on purpose —
> SetForge will plan a taper toward a meet, but it will never accidentally taper
> your swimmers toward a banquet. I'll anchor the meet to the Senior Squad, and it
> suggests a training phase from how many weeks out we are.

**A9 — Exports**
> SetForge plays nicely with the tools clubs already use. From the roster tab, one
> click gives you a meet-entry file in Hy-Tek Meet Manager format — there's our team
> code, gender spelled out, dates formatted the way the software expects.
> And here's the team calendar. You get a live link families can subscribe to, so
> their calendar updates automatically as your schedule changes. If a link ever gets
> shared too widely, you can regenerate it — and we'll warn you clearly before you
> do, because it resets everyone's subscription.

**A10 — Wrap**
> And that's it. We went from an empty account to a real team — roster imported, a
> workout assigned to the group, a typed schedule with a taper anchor, and exports
> ready for meet day. A few minutes, start to finish.

# Run B — Lesson-tier Coach

**B1 — Coach home (lesson tier)**
> If you teach lessons rather than run a club, SetForge has a tier just for you. Your
> home focuses on your swimmers, your small groups, and your own sets. The heavier
> club tools, like team administration and reports, stay out of your way until you
> need them.

**B2 — Managed swimmer detail**
> Here's a swimmer profile. I can set their ability level — beginner, intermediate,
> or advanced — and their own equipment, like fins or a kickboard. SetForge uses
> those to tailor what it generates for this specific swimmer. Parents are linked
> here too, and only the people you authorize can see a swimmer's details.

**B3 — Lesson groups**
> Small-group lessons work the same way — one group, one workout, everyone covered.

**B4 — Generate a lesson**
> When I choose the Lesson type, the workout changes shape — a warm-up, a skill
> focus, and a send-off, sized for a teaching session rather than a training set. I
> can pick the level, or let it read the swimmer's level automatically, and even
> limit it to just my own sets.

**B5 — My Sets**
> Speaking of which — I can author my own sets, tag them as lessons, and mark a
> level. Those become building blocks the generator reaches for.

**B6 — Parent recap**
> And after a lesson, one click sends the parent a clean recap of what their child
> worked on. It goes straight to the guardian on file — keeping parents informed is
> built in, not an afterthought.

**B7 — Wrap**
> From a four-year-old's first lesson to a masters swimmer, SetForge adapts — and
> keeps families in the loop the whole way.

# Run C — Swimmer

**C1 — Home**
> As a swimmer, SetForge greets you by name and, if you're on a team, shows your next
> event right up top, so you always know what's coming.

**C2 — Generate your own workout**
> Heading to the pool solo? Tell SetForge your level and what you want to work on,
> and it builds a complete workout in seconds — no staring at a blank whiteboard.

**C3 — Join a group**
> If your coach gives you a join code, you can drop it in here and you're connected
> to the group — no back-and-forth, no sharing personal details you don't want to.

**C4 — Log a workout**
> Finished a session? Log it. That's what powers your progress over time.

**C5 — Progress dashboard**
> Here's your progress — your training volume trending over the weeks, and your
> personal bests. It's your data, and you can export all of it, or delete your
> account entirely, any time you want.

**C6 — Team calendar**
> And you can download your team's schedule straight into your own calendar app, so
> practices and meets just show up alongside the rest of your life.

**C7 — Wrap**
> Whether you're with a team or training on your own, SetForge keeps your swimming
> organized and yours.

---

# Run D — Parent

**D1 — Parent view**
> For parents, SetForge keeps it simple. You see your swimmers, and a weekly summary
> of what they've been doing in the water — without needing to chase the coach.

**D2 — Per-swimmer digest**
> Pick a swimmer and you get their recent activity at a glance. You're seeing your
> own child's information, and only yours — access is tightly scoped to the families
> a swimmer actually belongs to.

**D3 — Team calendar**
> You can download the team's schedule right into your calendar. And notice what's in
> that file — practices and events, dates and places. What's deliberately not in
> there: no swimmer names, no contact details, nothing that could identify a child.
> Sharing a schedule should never mean sharing the kids.

**D4 — Digest controls**
> Prefer fewer emails? You're in control — pause the weekly digest with one toggle,
> any time.

**D5 — Wrap**
> SetForge gives parents real visibility, the way it should be — open, transparent,
> and built around keeping young athletes safe.
